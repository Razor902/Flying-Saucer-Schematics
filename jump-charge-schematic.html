PHYSICS BOUNDARY
The distribution, monitoring, contactors, interlocks, pulse forming, and lockout logic use conventional engineering patterns. A spacetime jump and the energy needed to produce one remain speculative; this drawing does not claim a working jump mechanism or construction-ready ratings.

Two sequencers. One irreversible moment.

Charging is staged and interruptible. Commit is all-or-nothing: any failed final poll sends the stored energy to the qualified dump path.

Charge cycle · JCS–20

  1. Run pre-charge checksProve containment, bank isolation, dump availability, cooling, instrumentation, and no active 86 lockout.
  2. Select one bankRotation logic assigns CHG–A, –B, or –C while the other banks remain isolated and monitored.
  3. Bulk chargeRCS–10 applies controlled constant current; voltage, temperature, and insulation are watched independently.
  4. Enter saturation taperReduce charge current near the validated bank limit. A missing taper is an overspeed condition.
  5. Balance the bankCorrect internal imbalance without connecting any other bank to the jump bus.
  6. Hold isolatedOpen the charging contactor, confirm the bank stable, and leave DMP–50 continuously available.

MANUAL ABORT IS AVAILABLE AT EVERY STEP → isolate source → open bank contactor → dump as required → latch 86 when trip criteria are met.

Jump commit · JCS–21

  1. Poll every final interlockContainment proven, nav solution locked, coupler impedance in band, drive bay clear, no active trip.
  2. Arm the dump branchVerify DMP–50 can accept the charge before any bank paralleling contactor closes.
  3. Parallel qualified banksClose independent contactors only after voltage agreement and isolation checks pass.
  4. Charge the pulse-forming networkTransfer energy into PSN–30 while rechecking the coupler and pre-fire current.
  5. Commit onceFire the shaped discharge through JC–40. No partial-fire command and no retry inside the same commit.
  6. Prove de-energizedOpen all contactors, discharge residual energy, verify safe potential, and inspect before another cycle.

BEFORE COMMIT: any failed poll opens the source and dumps. AFTER COMMIT: protection opens all available paths and latches 86; it never commands a second pulse.

Five votes required to fire.

These are hard permissives, not advisory alarms. Losing any vote removes the commit permit.

Trip schedule · 86 lockout

Trips never self-clear. Restoring a normal reading does not restore the machine; a human must inspect the cause and walk the reset checklist.

TRIP JC–01 · 86 LOCKOUT

Charge imbalance

TRIGGER — disagreement between bank sections beyond the validated balance envelope, or loss of a balance measurement.

  1. Block the charging supply.
  2. Open the affected bank contactor.
  3. Route the affected bank to DMP–50 if its monitored state requires discharge.
  4. Inhibit paralleling and jump commit.

MANUAL RESET — inspect bank sections, prove measurement integrity, complete a controlled balance, verify safe potential, then reset 86–JC01 locally.

TRIP JC–02 · 86 LOCKOUT

Pre-fire current

TRIGGER — current appears in the discharge path while armed but before the commit command, indicating a leaking or failed path.

  1. Open all bank paralleling contactors.
  2. Block PSN–30 firing.
  3. Command immediate dump through DMP–50.
  4. Record the first-out contact.

MANUAL RESET — isolate and test the discharge path, contactors, gate controls, and current sensors; prove zero-energy state before resetting 86–JC02.

TRIP JC–03 · 86 LOCKOUT

Coupler impedance out of band

TRIGGER — JC–40 impedance leaves its validated window during final poll, PSN transfer, or armed hold.

  1. Withdraw the commit permit.
  2. Block the pulse-shaping gate.
  3. Open bank contactors.
  4. Discharge PSN–30 and banks to the dump network.

MANUAL RESET — inspect coupler, cabling, terminations, and measurement channel; prove impedance with an independent test before resetting 86–JC03.

TRIP JC–04 · 86 LOCKOUT

Dump-path unavailable

TRIGGER — open dump continuity, loss of sink capacity indication, contactor disagreement, or dump path not proven.

  1. Inhibit further charging immediately.
  2. Prevent bank paralleling and commit.
  3. Isolate each charged bank individually.
  4. Maintain monitoring from the protected control supply.

MANUAL RESET — repair and prove the full path from each bank through DMP–50 to RS–51 under the approved test method, then reset 86–JC04.

TRIP JC–05 · 86 LOCKOUT

Charging supply overspeed

TRIGGER — charging rate fails to follow command, current limit is exceeded, or the required saturation taper does not occur.

  1. Trip the RCS–10 input contactor.
  2. Open the selected bank’s charging contactor.
  3. Evaluate bank temperature and voltage.
  4. Dump if the bank cannot remain safely isolated.

MANUAL RESET — test regulation, current limit, command feedback, and taper control; verify the bank before resetting 86–JC05.
